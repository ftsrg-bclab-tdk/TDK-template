\section{Process terms}
{\small

  \toprule \textbf{Term} & \textbf{Meaning} \\ \midrule \endfirsthead
  \toprule \textbf{Term} & \textbf{Meaning} \\ \midrule \endhead
  \bottomrule \endlastfoot
  Topic manifest & One page approved before the semester: the problem, why it matters, the expected contribution. \\
  Literature review & A search for sources, logged in one notes file with its search strings, databases and dates; there is no screening protocol. It gives the gap claim a documented set of sources. \\
  Gap analysis & Finding what existing approaches cannot do. Produces the positioning figure, gap matrix and a one-sentence gap. \\
  Gap matrix & Requirements compared against approaches in \tplref{ch:related}; see \cref{guide:chapters:existing-approaches}. \\
  Positioning figure & Related work plotted on two axes with three levels each (\tplref{fig:positioning}); see \cref{guide:chapters:existing-approaches}. \\
  Leading figure (vezérábra) & The one figure that explains the work; see \cref{guide:writing:figure}. \\
  Spine & One page fixing the paper's claims, terminology and notation; see \cref{guide:writing:spine}. \\
  Skeleton mind map & Each chapter planned as paragraph topic sentences with supporting evidence, before drafting prose; see \cref{guide:writing:spine}. \\
  Dependency graph & The skeleton's paragraphs with an arrow to each paragraph that needs them, the contribution in green; see \cref{guide:writing:depgraph}. \\
  Beats & What each planned paragraph establishes, its evidence, its use of the example and its link to the next paragraph. \\
  Essentials & The elements every paper of this kind contains, measured by the lint on every compile; see \cref{guide:writing:essentials}. \\
  Premortem & Predicting why the paper might score poorly on each criterion, then addressing those objections before drafting. TDK papers receive placements rather than acceptance or rejection. \\
  Anchor & An artifact we approve before the next phase and use to check later work: the topic manifest and the spine, one page each, and the full draft, approved at a revision recorded in the consultation summary; see \cref{guide:lifecycle}. \\
  Drift & Work that contradicts an anchor without re-opening it; see \cref{guide:lifecycle}. \\
  Official step & A step of the faculty's TDK with a portal date: registration with the abstract, paper upload, the conference; see \cref{guide:tdk:dates}. \\
  Department session & The writing session before the deadline and the presentation rehearsal before the conference, organised by the department for all its TDK students; see \cref{guide:tab:lifecycle}. \\
  Background material & Our papers, notes and earlier work on the topic, which we put into \texttt{notes/} of your notes repository in the first week. \\
  Hill climb & Repeatedly assessing work and fixing its weakest point, whether proofs, implementation or, in the review sheet hill climb, the paper against the review sheet; \cref{guide:tab:lifecycle} says when each is done. \\
  Reviewer pass & An agent's reading as a simulated reviewer with a narrow brief, before real review; see \cref{guide:lifecycle:reviewers}. \\
  Skim view & The reviewer PDF reduced to headings, labelled items, boxes, figures and captions, with paragraph lengths replacing prose; paper CI builds it on main-branch pushes, non-draft pull requests and manual runs, see \cref{guide:lifecycle:reviewers}. \\
  Requirements & Every requirement in the guide, with its source, its check and its due phase, including lint rules (\cref{guide:code:paperci}); see \cref{guide:tab:polish}. \\
  Burn-down & Counts of todos, beats, placeholder figures and TODO cells, which paper CI reports on those same runs and the lifecycle CI records (\cref{guide:code:paperci}); \cref{guide:tab:polish} says when they reach zero. \\
  Reviewer PDF & The paper built from \texttt{reviewer.tex}: no notes, no guide; the file to upload. \\
  Results tag & The Git tag and Zenodo version used for the paper's numbers; see \cref{guide:zenodo:cite,guide:zenodo:versions}. \\
  Version DOI, concept DOI & Identifiers for one archived release and for all versions together, respectively; \cref{guide:zenodo:cite} says which one the paper cites. \\

\section{Technical terms}
The common glossary, \texttt{include/glossary-common.tex}, declares the abbreviations, notation and terms our topics share (\cref{guide:tab:glossary-common}). Use them with \texttt{\textbackslash gls\{key\}} without declaring them: only the ones your text uses appear in your lists. To change an entry for your paper, declare the same key in \texttt{content/glossary.tex}, and yours replaces it. The template's running example and the tools of our topics are not in the common glossary; \cref{guide:tab:glossary-technical} explains them.
